The Cost of Getting Sick: Global Health Progress and the Price of Care
By Safia Ulysse

### 1. What I have done so far

So far, I have developed the structure and visual design of my webpage using HTML, CSS, and D3.js. I have organized the project around the **AND-BUT-THEREFORE** storytelling approach to explore global health progress and the financial challenges that still exist. In the first scene, I created four interactive line charts showing trends in extreme poverty, universal health coverage, life expectancy, and health expenditure, with options to compare regions and income groups. In the second scene, I developed two animated bar charts showing catastrophic health spending and further impoverishment caused by out-of-pocket healthcare payments. I also added interactive legends, coordinated highlighting, and controls that allow users to explore changes over time.

### 2. What will be the most challenging part of my project to design, and why?

The most challenging part will be designing the third scene and connecting it clearly to the first two scenes. I want to explore how different ways of financing healthcare may help explain why improvements in health coverage do not always lead to financial protection. I also hope to include a successful country case study where a healthcare reform or policy helped improve outcomes and reduce the financial burden on households. However, the World Bank dataset does not provide enough information to fully evaluate the impact of a specific reform, so I am considering using an external source, such as a published research study, to present its findings. Another challenge will be combining these different sources into a clear and engaging visualization while making sure I do not imply a causal relationship that the data cannot support.

01 / THE PROGRESS

A World Moving Forward

Over the past two decades, the world has made remarkable progress. Extreme poverty has declined, access to essential health services has expanded, and people are living longer. Yet progress has not followed the same path everywhere, and healthcare spending represents a very different share of national economies across regions and income groups. But has progress been equal everywhere?

02 / THE HIDDEN COST OF CARE

Better Access, But at What Cost?

Access to essential health services has expanded, but paying for healthcare can still place a heavy financial burden on households. For some people, out-of-pocket payments consume more than a quarter of household resources. For those already living in poverty, these payments can push them even further below the poverty line. How much financial hardship remains today, and how has it changed since 2000?
